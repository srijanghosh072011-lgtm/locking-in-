7 Signs You Need a Water Heater Repair — or a New One

Water heaters rarely die without warning. Here are the seven signals worth acting on — before the cold-shower morning.

Quick answer

The seven warning signs of a failing water heater are: water that's lukewarm or runs out fast, rusty or discoloured hot water, rumbling or popping noises, moisture or drips around the tank base, a pilot that won't stay lit, rising gas bills, and age past 8–10 years. Leaks from the tank body itself always mean replacement; most other symptoms are repairable if the tank is under 8 years old.

1. The hot water isn't hot — or runs out fast

A sudden drop usually means a failed heating element (electric) or a sediment-buried burner (gas). In Regina's hard water, sediment builds quickly and insulates the water from the flame. Often fixable with a flush and part swap if the tank is younger than 8 years.

2. Rusty or discoloured hot water

Brown or rusty water from the hot side only points at the tank's sacrificial anode rod being spent — or the tank itself corroding. An anode swap around year 5 is cheap; rust after year 10 usually means the tank is finishing its life.

3. Rumbling, popping or banging

That's water flash-boiling under a layer of hardened sediment. It stresses the tank, wastes gas, and is the classic "flush me" signal. Ignore it long enough and the overheating shortens tank life sharply.

4. Moisture or drips around the base

Check the fittings and the temperature-and-pressure relief valve first — both are repairable. But if the tank body itself is weeping, that's terminal: no repair fixes a corroded-through tank. Replace before it becomes a flood.

5. The pilot light won't stay lit

Usually a failed thermocouple — a quick, inexpensive repair. On newer units, repeated lockouts can indicate venting or gas-valve issues that need a licensed gas fitter.

6. Gas or power bills creeping up

An aging, sediment-laden heater works harder for the same hot water. If your usage hasn't changed but the bill has, the water heater is a prime suspect.

7. It's simply old

Standard tanks in Regina's hard water typically last 8–12 years (the serial number encodes the manufacture date — we can decode it in seconds). Past year 10, budget for replacement on your schedule rather than the tank's.

Repair or replace? The one-third rule

If a repair costs more than a third of a comparable new installation and the tank is past 8 years old, put the money toward replacement. Anything leaking from the tank shell replaces regardless of age. And if you're replacing anyway, it's the perfect moment to consider a tankless upgrade.

Straight answers

Related Questions

How long should a water heater last in Regina?
8–12 years for a standard tank — Regina's hard water pushes most tanks toward the shorter end unless they're flushed annually and the anode rod is replaced around year 5. Tankless units last 15–20 years with maintenance.
Is a leaking water heater an emergency?
Treat it as one. A weeping tank can progress to a full rupture that releases 150+ litres plus continuous inflow. Shut off the cold supply valve above the tank and the gas/power, then call a plumber the same day.
Why does my hot water smell like rotten eggs?
Sulphate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode rod. It's harmless but unpleasant — swapping to an aluminum-zinc anode and sanitizing the tank usually eliminates the odour.
