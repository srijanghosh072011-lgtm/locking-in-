How to Prevent Frozen Pipes in a Regina Winter

Regina regularly sees -30°C and colder. Here's the checklist our plumbers use on their own homes.

Quick answer

To prevent frozen pipes in Regina: keep the furnace no lower than 16°C even when away, open cabinet doors on exterior-wall plumbing during cold snaps, disconnect hoses and drain outdoor taps every fall, seal rim-joist drafts near pipes, and let a pencil-thin stream run overnight during extreme cold warnings. Pipes in garages, crawl spaces and along north-facing exterior walls freeze first.

Why frozen pipes are a Regina problem, not a plumbing problem

Water expands about 9% when it freezes. Trapped in a closed pipe, that expansion generates enormous pressure — more than enough to split copper and crack fittings. The burst usually shows itself during the thaw, when the ice plug melts and water pours through the split. The Insurance Bureau of Canada consistently reports water damage as the leading cause of home insurance claims in the country, and winter pipe failures are a big share of the Prairie portion.

Which pipes freeze first?

  • Outdoor hose bibs with a hose still attached (the water can't drain back).
  • Lines through unheated garages — especially laundry or hose lines in garage ceilings.
  • Pipes on exterior walls, particularly north-facing kitchen sinks.
  • Crawl spaces and rim joists where cold air leaks onto plumbing.
  • Homes left empty with the thermostat set too low during holidays.

The prevention checklist

Every fall (before Halloween)

  • Disconnect garden hoses and drain or shut off exterior taps from inside.
  • Have frost-free hose bibs checked if they dripped last season.
  • Seal drafts at the rim joist with foam or caulk anywhere pipes run nearby.
  • Insulate accessible pipe runs in crawl spaces and garages with foam sleeves.

During extreme cold warnings (-30°C and colder)

  • Open the cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes.
  • Let the most vulnerable cold tap run at a pencil-thin trickle overnight. Moving water resists freezing, and the few cents of water is cheap insurance.
  • Keep the garage door closed if any plumbing runs through the garage.

If you leave town in winter

  • Never set the thermostat below 16°C, and ask someone to check the house every couple of days — many insurance policies require it for coverage to apply.
  • For longer absences, shut the main valve and drain the system, or have us winterize the home properly.

What to do if a pipe freezes anyway

  1. Find the main shut-off valve first — if the pipe has split, you'll want it closed the moment thawing starts.
  2. Open the affected tap so melting water and steam can escape.
  3. Warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or heating pad, working from the tap toward the frozen section.
  4. Never use an open flame. Torches cause house fires and can flash-boil trapped water, bursting the pipe explosively.
  5. No flow after 30–45 minutes, or the frozen section is inside a wall? Stop and call us — we thaw lines safely with controlled equipment and can check for splits before repressurizing.
Straight answers

Related Questions

At what temperature do pipes freeze in a house?
Pipes in unheated or poorly insulated spaces are at risk once sustained outdoor temperatures drop below about -6°C, but in Regina the real danger zone is extreme cold snaps of -25°C and below combined with wind, which drives freezing air into wall cavities and rim joists.
Should I let my taps drip in extreme cold?
Yes — during extreme cold warnings, run the most vulnerable cold-water tap at a pencil-thin trickle overnight. Moving water is far harder to freeze, and the cost is negligible compared to a burst-pipe claim.
What temperature should I leave my thermostat at when away in winter?
No lower than 16°C in Regina, with interior doors and sink cabinets left open. Check your home insurance policy too — most require regular house checks while you're away for water-damage coverage to remain valid.
