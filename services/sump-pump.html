Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Regina, SK

Regina's spring melt and summer cloudbursts don't negotiate. A properly sized pump with battery backup — and a backwater valve on the sewer — is what keeps basements dry here.

Quick answer

Plumbo installs, repairs and replaces sump pumps and battery backup systems across Regina, and installs backwater valves that protect basements from sewer backup during heavy rain. Backwater valve installations may qualify for a City of Regina flood-protection rebate; we handle the paperwork, sizing, permits and inspection.

Regina basements flood two ways: groundwater rising during spring melt (a sump pump problem) and the city sewer surcharging during intense storms (a backwater valve problem). Full protection means addressing both — a pump alone won't stop sewage arriving through the floor drain.

Pumps fail silently: the switch sticks, the check valve leaks back, or the power goes out in the same storm that floods the pit. That's why every install we recommend includes a test schedule, and why battery backup is the best few hundred dollars a Regina homeowner can spend.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the City of Regina offer a rebate for backwater valves?
The City of Regina has run flood-protection rebate programs covering a portion of backwater valve and sump pump installation costs for eligible homes. Program details and funding change year to year — we confirm current eligibility and complete the paperwork with you at quote time.
How often should I replace my sump pump?
Plan on 7–10 years for a quality primary pump, sooner if it cycles heavily each spring. Test it every spring by lifting the float; if it's over 7 years old and matters to your basement, replace it proactively — always before the melt, not after.
Do I need a battery backup if I have a good pump?
In Regina, yes. The storms that flood basements are the same storms that knock out power, and a dead outlet makes the best pump ornamental. A battery backup runs hours of pumping and alarms when it activates.
What is a backwater valve and do I need one?
It's a one-way flap installed on your main sewer line that lets wastewater out but blocks city sewage from surging back in during storm surcharge. For homes with finished basements — especially in lower-lying Regina neighbourhoods — it's the single most effective sewer-backup protection available.
