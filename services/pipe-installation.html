Pipe Installation & Repiping in Regina, SK

From one new line for a basement bathroom to a whole-home repipe that finally retires 60-year-old galvanized — planned tidy, done to code.

Quick answer

We run new water and drain lines and do whole-home repiping across Regina, swapping out failing galvanized steel and poly-b for PEX or copper. Most full repipes take 2–4 days, we turn the water back on every evening so you can stay in the house, we keep the drywall openings small and mapped, and the permits and inspection are included.

A lot of Regina homes are still running on their original galvanized steel (anything pre-1960s) or poly-b (late-70s to mid-90s). Galvanized rusts shut from the inside — that's the sad little trickle in the upstairs shower — and poly-b likes to let go at the fittings with no warning at all. Insurers have caught on and are surcharging or flat-out refusing to cover both.

Repiping sounds like we're tearing your house apart. Done right, we're not. We plan the fewest access holes we can get away with, keep the water on every night, photograph everything for your records, and leave you with new lines, real pressure again, and often a cheaper insurance renewal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it cost to repipe a house?
Most Regina bungalows come in around $6,000–$12,000 in PEX, depending on size, finishes and how easy the walls are to get into; two-storeys and copper run higher. We give you a fixed price after a walkthrough, and that includes patching the access holes rough-ready for paint.
How long does a whole-home repipe take?
Usually 2–4 days for an average house. The water's only off during working hours and back on every evening, so you carry on living there the whole time.
PEX or copper — what should I go with?
PEX is our default: it flexes instead of splitting when it freezes (a real perk in Saskatchewan), it's quieter, faster to run and cheaper. Copper still earns its keep on exposed runs, near high heat, and where you want that solid finished look.
My poly-b hasn't leaked — do I really need to replace it?
Poly-b lets go without warning, usually right at the fittings, and a lot of insurers now surcharge, limit or flat-out refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replacing it on your own schedule beats replacing it mid-flood.
